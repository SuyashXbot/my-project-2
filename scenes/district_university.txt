Outside the western river-wall across the muddy ferry of the [b]Aur[/b] sprawls [b]The University of the Scholiasts[/b], a turbulent liberty of ink-stained gowns, Latin disputations, and flying cobblestones. Here five hundred clerks and tonsured scholars lodge four to a truckle-bed inside the timber-framed chambers of [b]Sparrow Hostel[/b] and [b]St. Clement's Hostel[/b], while wealthier fellows dine on salted ling behind the stone quadrangles of [b]King's Hall College[/b] and [b]The College of the Decretals[/b]. Along [b]Straw Street[/b]—named for the rushes strewn ankle-deep across the lecture-floors—stationers and illuminators display chained folios in the open shutters of [b]The Brazen Serpent Bookshop[/b] and [b]Vellum Lane[/b]. The air smells of wet sheepskin, cheap red wine spilled from [b]The Mitre Tavern[/b], and boiled cabbage drifting from [b]Scholars' Well[/b] toward the disputing schools of [b]Canonists' Row[/b]. When the curfew bell tolls from [b]Saint Severin-by-the-Ferry[/b], the Chancellor's bedels patrol with silver maces to stop tavern brawls between northern and southern nations, passing the carved stone cross above [b]Plague-Scholars' Close[/b], where three entire halls of masters perished in the Great Pestilence and left their glossed Latin books without heirs.
