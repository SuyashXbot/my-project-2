South of Cathedral Isle, the nineteen stone arches of [b]The Great Bridge[/b] span the roaring tide of the [b]Aurthel[/b], its roadway choked by four-story timber-framed jettied houses and haberdashers' booths that groan above the water-wheels. Where the western bridge-head touches the marshy bank sprawls [b]Bridgefoot and the Stews[/b], the mud-drowned sanctuary of the city's poorest laborers, washerwomen, bear-wards, and outlaws. Along [b]Cock Lane[/b] and [b]Maidenhead Alley[/b], whitewashed bath-houses and low tippling-cellars under the sign of [b]The Boar's Tusk Tavern[/b] and [b]The Cardinal's Hat[/b] lean over ditches clogged with tanners' lime, dead dogs, and butchered sheep-heads. Beside the iron-spiked gatehouse of [b]Bridgefoot Gaol[/b], debtors and branded felons beg through rusted bars at passersby drawing brackish water from [b]Leper's Well[/b], across from the sagging porch of [b]Saint Mary-Over-the-Water[/b]. Above the southern draw-pier stands the noseless, filth-smeared stone effigy of the reviled [b]King Vortimer II[/b], while at the lane's end the black turf of [b]Deadman's Ditch[/b] marks the great plague-pit where ten thousand nameless poor were tumbled without shrouds when the Great Pestilence swept the river-shacks.
