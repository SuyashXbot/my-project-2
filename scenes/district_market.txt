At the loud, reeking navel of the walled island spreads [b]The Great Market Square[/b], where three days a week five thousand buyers and country hucksters jostle axle-to-axle between the timber-framed jettied tenements of [b]Poultry Row[/b] and [b]Shambles Lane[/b]. Blood and offal from the butchers' blocks run down the central stone gutter into [b]Great Conduit[/b], mingling with the fragrance of bruised cloves from [b]Spicers' Arcade[/b], boiling lard from [b]Pie-Corner[/b], and wet wool piled before the [b]Mercers' Hall[/b]. Above the trestle stalls towers the freestone porch of the [b]City Guildhall[/b], where aldermen in furred scarlet gowns hold the Court of Piepowder beside the pillory and the iron chains of [b]The Tun Prison[/b]. In the center of the cobbles rises the octagonal steps of [b]The Market Cross[/b], crowned by the ancient bronze-and-limestone statue of [b]King Aurelian I the Founder[/b], while across the square the bells of [b]Saint Bartholomew-at-the-Stocks[/b] ring the market hours when forestallers may not buy before common citizens. Behind [b]Butter-Cross Well[/b], two boarded-up tenements still bear the carved date of the Great Pestilence, when cattle wandered untended among the spilled bolts of Flanders cloth.
